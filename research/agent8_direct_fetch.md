## Findings matched to the tree

1. **Marcos Valiente Muñasque (G2, row 949) was Municipal President of Medellin, Cebu, 1923–1933.**
   The Freeman (Philstar), "The officials of Medellin, Cebu in the 1930s" (5 Sep 2016) lists the municipal
   presidents: "... Pedro Perez (1919-1922), Marcos Munasque (1923-1933), Jose Ancajas (1933-1940) ...".
   The same columnist's "Pedro Perez, Municipal President of Medellin, Cebu" (7 Feb 2016) says "Perez was
   succeeded in 1923 by Marcos Munasque". Identity: the spreadsheet's Marcos married Matilde Perez; his
   predecessor was Pedro Perez (an in-law link is possible but unproven); his sister Agripina's Salimbangon
   family lived in Curva, Medellin; his son Col. Coronado "Stim" Muñasque built a resort at Kawit, Medellin
   in 2002. Confidence: moderate-high (rare surname, right town, right generation).
2. **Cosmiano Valiente Muñasque (G2, row 1746) was a councilor of Kolambugan, Lanao, 1945–1946.**
   Official Gazette, "Appointments and Designations: August, 1946" (read on the Wayback Machine): "R. P.
   Tumala, Isabelo Enriquez, Martin Comos, Cosmeniano Muñasque, Quirino Asupre and Fortunato Somontan,
   appointed Councilors of Kolambugan, Lanao, July 12, 1946." A search snippet of the January 1946 list adds
   "Cosmiano Munasque" among acting councilors of Kolambugan appointed 14 December 1945 (page not archived;
   verify). This documents the Cosmiano branch on Panguil Bay, where the family's largest cluster lives today.
   Confidence: moderate-high.
3. **Col. Coronado "Stim" Perez Muñasque (G3, row 1030).** Philstar, 15 Sep 2005: SEC criminal complaint
   names "directors Coronado Munasque, Sen. Juan Flavier, ..." of College Assurance Plans Phils. Inc.
   Supreme Court G.R. No. 169444 (17 Sep 2008), Villarin v. Munasque: Coronado P. Munasque sued on
   10 July 2002 in Makati to collect P15 million, secured by 34 land titles in Tanauan City. Medellin travel
   guide (2012): his private resort at Kawit, Medellin (2002), now Seahorse Tourist Inn. Middle initial P.
   (Perez) matches. Confidence: moderate-high. No military record was found for the "Col." title.
4. **Atty. Benjamin N. Muñasque (G4, row 1530).** Three Supreme Court administrative matters now: A.M.
   No. P-227 (30 Sep 1974, he as complainant), A.M. No. P-145 (31 Oct 1974, he as respondent) and A.M. No.
   P-1337 (Jan 1980, letters of 2 Aug 1976 and 7 Mar 1978 routed through "Atty. Benjamin Muñasque, Clerk of
   Court, CFI, Cotabato City"). He was Clerk of Court, Branch I, Court of First Instance of Cotabato, at
   least 1972–1978.
5. **Elmo D. Muñasque (G3, row 1912).** G.R. No. L-39780 read in full: the contract "made this 20th day of
   December in the year 1966 by Galan and Muñasque" for remodelling Tropical Commercial Co.'s Cebu branch;
   the partnership "Galan and Associates" held a Cebu City construction permit. Name match only.
6. **Gabriel D. Muñasque (G5, row 1601).** ICTSI news confirms the quote "said Gabriel Muñasque, SCIPSI
   general manager" (50th anniversary); G.R. No. 235569 concerns union dues of 2006–2007. Name and place
   match only.
7. **Fr. Rosalio N. Muñasque (G4, row 1665).** Pages re-read; nothing new beyond S21–S24.
8. **Sulpicio Y. Muñasque (G3, row 1720).** UCCP Lagao slideshow re-read; wording of S29 confirmed.

## Round 8b: the two Opon graves (Cristina and Leonora P. Munasque)

Asked whether these two women are family, the following was tried (28 Oct 2026, later session):

- **The grave pages themselves.** Find a Grave blocks automated readers, but asking the Wayback Machine to
  archive each page worked. Both memorials (240661312 Crisitina Munasque, 1930–2006; 240661319 Leonora P.
  Munasque, 1944–2012) were created on 15 June 2022 by the contributor "Lgcs", carry no photograph, no
  inscription, no plot number and no family links, and photo requests are disabled for the cemetery. They
  look like transcriptions from a burial register, so the names and dates are probably reliable but they
  name no relatives. (The memorial numbers next to theirs belong to unrelated graves elsewhere, so the
  "neighbouring graves" idea gave nothing.)
- **The same cemetery, other family surnames.** The cemetery's own search (Lapu-Lapu Municipal Cemetery,
  Find a Grave cemetery 2755028) lists no Muñasque beyond these two, no Gusat, Ynot, Etcuban, Umpad,
  Valiente or Lloren; 2 Cosep (Benita B. Cosep 1941–2010, Flaviana Cosep 1949–2000) and 18 Patalinghug
  (among them Ananias B. 1916–1999, Conrado T. 1912–1979, Carlito 1944–1984, Elizabeth 1938–2000, Eduardo
  1945–2008). None of these names is in the spreadsheet either.
- **The tree.** No Cristina, Kristina or Tina Muñasque, and no Muñasque man's wife named Cristina or
  Leonora. The only Leonora Muñasque (Leonora G., daughter of Asisclo Gusat Muñasque, m. Damian Lloren,
  Polomolok) has a different middle initial and lived in Mindanao. Branches whose children would carry a
  middle initial P.: Cruz Valiente Muñasque m. Remedios Dela Piña (only two recorded children, so possibly
  incomplete) and Gideon A. Muñasque Sr. m. Elsa Guarde Paredes (children probably born after 1944).
- **Web search** for either name in any spelling: nothing.
- **Time-sensitive notice.** SunStar Cebu (Oct 2026): Lapu-Lapu City will exhume 791 remains buried five
  years or more at the municipal cemetery in Sitio Tugas, Barangay Gun-ob on 15 November 2026; families
  have until 14 December 2026 to claim remains through the City Health Office (032-340-5339; sanitary
  inspector Benjie Celera 0932-973-0753), after which unclaimed remains go to a common bone chamber. Both
  women were buried there more than five years ago. The City Health Office keeps the burial register that
  names the next of kin, which is the one record that would settle who they were.

Conclusion: not placeable from online sources. Verdict stays "unplaced namesakes of Opon" until a
relative, or the City Health Office burial register, names their parents or husbands.
